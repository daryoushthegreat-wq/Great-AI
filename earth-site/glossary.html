Glossary

126 geology terms used in this guide.

ABCDEFGHIJLMNOPQRSTUVW

A

Alfred Wegener
The German scientist who explained the theory of continental drift in the early 1900s. Drifting Continents
Alps
A mountain chain in Europe. The main squeezes that formed it happened about 25 million years ago. Mountains
ammonite
An extinct sea animal with a coiled shell, often found as a fossil in limestone. Strata
ancient mountains
The worn-down remains of much higher mountains, such as the Scottish Highlands, the Urals and the Appalachians. Mountains
Andes
The volcano-studded mountains of South America. They rose as an ocean plate slid beneath the continent. Mountains
andesite
Volcanic rock from lava that is lighter than basalt. It is common where one plate sinks under another. Volcanoes
arid
Very dry, with little or no rain. The Moving Map
Atlantic Ocean
The ocean between the Americas and Europe and Africa. Similar rocks and fossils on both sides show the continents were once joined. Drifting Continents
atmosphere
The layer of gases held around a planet by its gravity. How the Earth Was Formed
atom
The tiniest particle of an element that can exist. The World of Crystals

B

basalt
Dark volcanic rock formed from runny lava. It wells up at mid-ocean ridges and hotspots. Volcanoes
borehole
A narrow, deep hole drilled into the ground to study or extract rock, water or oil. The Outer Layers
brittle
Hard but easily broken or cracked, rather than bending. Earthquakes
buckle
To bend and crumple under pressure, as rock layers do when squeezed for a long time. The Outer Layers

C

calcite
The mineral that chalk is made of, almost entirely. The World of Crystals
Cenozoic
The era of "new life", from 66 million years ago to today. The Geological Timescale
chalk
A soft white sedimentary rock formed from the skeletons of tiny sea plants. Strata
climate
The usual weather of a place over a long time. The Moving Map
coal
A black rock that burns, formed from ancient forests buried and compressed under layers of mud and sand. Treasures from the Earth
coal seam
A layer of coal in rock, made from the compressed remains of ancient forests. The Moving Map
coastline
The edge where land meets sea. It moves slowly as land and sea levels change.
compound
A substance made of two or more elements joined together chemically. The World of Crystals
continental crust
The thick, light crust that forms the continents. Plates
continental drift
The very slow movement of the continents across the earth’s surface, carried on moving plates. Drifting Continents
continental shelf
The gently sloping edge of a continent, covered by shallow sea. The Moving Map
crater
A bowl-shaped hollow made by an impact or by a volcano. How the Earth Was Formed
crust
The very thin top layer of the earth’s shell. It is cooler than the rock below. Inside the Earth
crystal
A solid whose atoms are fixed together in a regular, repeating pattern. The World of Crystals

D

density
How heavy a material is for its size. Light continental rock floats on denser rock below. Mountains
deposit
To lay down sediment in a new place. The material that is laid down is also called a deposit. The Outer Layers
diameter
A straight line across a circle or ball, passing through its centre. The earth’s diameter is over 12,700 km. Inside the Earth
diamond
The hardest natural mineral. Its crystals are found in rock that was once molten, deep under the earth’s shell. Treasures from the Earth

E

element
A basic chemical substance that cannot be split into simpler substances. About a hundred are known, such as hydrogen, oxygen and iron. How the Earth Was Formed
era
A very long division of geological time, made up of several periods. The Geological Timescale
erosion
The wearing away and carrying off of rock and soil by wind, water and ice. Our Earth
eruption
The bursting out of lava, gas and ash from a volcano. Volcanoes

F

fault line
The line on the surface where a fault, a break in the rock, can be seen. Earthquakes
faulting
The cracking and slipping of rock along a break when it is squeezed or stretched. Rocks from Inside the Earth
feldspar
A common group of silicate minerals. Granite is a mixture of feldspar, quartz and other minerals. The World of Crystals
fjord
A long, deep, narrow sea inlet in a valley carved by ice. The Moving Map
fold mountains
Mountains formed when rock layers are squeezed between plates and buckle upwards. Mountains
folding
The bending of rock layers by heavy, slow, sideways pressure. Rocks from Inside the Earth
fossil
The preserved remains or traces of a plant or animal that lived long ago. Drifting Continents
fossil fuel
A fuel such as coal, oil or gas, formed over millions of years from the remains of living things. Treasures from the Earth

G

garnet
A mineral that grows as crystals inside metamorphic rock. It shows as dark spots in schist. Rocks from Inside the Earth
gemstone
A beautiful, rare mineral crystal that is cut and polished for jewellery. Treasures from the Earth
geological timescale
The calendar geologists use to divide the earth’s history into eras and periods. The Geological Timescale
geology
The science of rocks and of the earth’s history, which is recorded in its rocks. The Outer Layers
gneiss
A banded metamorphic rock that was folded deep inside the earth’s crust. Rocks from Inside the Earth
gravity
The force that pulls objects towards each other. Earth’s gravity holds the atmosphere and the oceans in place. How the Earth Was Formed

H

Himalayas
The mountain chain formed where the plate carrying India pressed against the plate carrying Asia. Plates
Holocene
The most recent part of the Quaternary period: the one we live in now. The Geological Timescale
hotspot
A place far from plate edges where magma rises from deep in the mantle, such as Hawaii. Volcanoes
hydrogen
The lightest element, a gas. Stars such as the Sun are made mostly of hydrogen. How the Earth Was Formed

I

ice age
A long cold period when ice sheets spread over much of the land. The Moving Map
igneous rock
Rock that formed when molten rock cooled and hardened. Granite is an igneous rock. Rocks from Inside the Earth
inner core
The solid ball of rock and metal at the very centre of the earth. Inside the Earth

J

jade
A tough green stone used for carving. It is made from the minerals nephrite and jadeite. Treasures from the Earth
Jurassic
The middle period of the Mesozoic era, when dinosaurs roamed the land. The Geological Timescale

L

landslide
A sudden fall of rock and soil down a slope. Earthquakes
lava
Molten rock that has reached the earth’s surface. Volcanoes
limestone
A sedimentary rock made mostly of calcite, often from shells and skeletons of sea life. Strata

M

magma
Molten rock churned up from the mantle, below the surface. Plates
mantle
The thick layer of white-hot rock, about 2,900 km deep, between the crust and the core. It is solid but churns very slowly. Inside the Earth
Mesozoic
The era of "middle life", from the Triassic to the Cretaceous. It was the age of the dinosaurs. The Geological Timescale
metamorphic rock
Rock that was re-made from existing rock by great heat and pressure, without melting. Rocks from Inside the Earth
meteorite
A piece of rock or metal from space that falls and reaches the ground. How the Earth Was Formed
mica
A flaky mineral that splits into very thin sheets. Rocks from Inside the Earth
mid-ocean ridge
A long underwater mountain chain where two plates pull apart and new ocean crust forms. Plates
mineral
A natural crystalline chemical. Rocks are made up of minerals. The World of Crystals
Moho
The boundary at the base of the crust. It was discovered by studying how earthquake waves travel. The Outer Layers
molten
Melted by intense heat. Molten rock is liquid rock. Our Earth
mountain range
A line or group of mountains formed by the same movements of the earth. Our Earth
mountain root
The thick part of the crust below a mountain chain. Like an iceberg, most of it is hidden beneath the surface. Mountains

N

nuclear reaction
A change in the centres of atoms that releases huge amounts of energy. In the Sun, nuclear reactions turn hydrogen into helium. How the Earth Was Formed

O

obsidian
A natural black glass made when granite magma cools very quickly. Volcanoes
ocean trench
A deep, narrow valley in the ocean floor where one plate is forced down into the mantle. Plates
oceanic crust
The thin, dense crust that forms the ocean floors. Plates
oil
A liquid fossil fuel formed mostly from the remains of tiny sea life. Treasures from the Earth
orbit
The curved path that one object follows around another, such as the earth around the Sun. How the Earth Was Formed
ore
A rock or mineral from which a useful metal can be extracted. The World of Crystals
outer core
The liquid layer around the earth’s centre, made largely of iron. Inside the Earth

P

Palaeozoic
The era of "ancient life", from the Cambrian to the Permian period. The Geological Timescale
Pangaea
The name scientists give to the single supercontinent that existed about 200 million years ago. Drifting Continents
peat
Soft, brown, partly rotted plant material. Under pressure, layers of peat become coal. Treasures from the Earth
period
A division of an era, such as the Jurassic period. The Geological Timescale
plain
A wide area of flat land. Over millions of years, even the highest mountains are worn down to plains. Mountains
plankton
Tiny plants and animals that drift in the sea. Their remains formed most of the world’s oil. Treasures from the Earth
plate
One of the rigid pieces that the earth’s shell is broken into. The plates fit together like a crazy jigsaw. Plates
Precambrian
All of the earth’s history before the Cambrian period. It is by far the longest part. The Geological Timescale
pumice
A very light, frothy volcanic rock full of gas bubbles. Volcanoes

Q

quarry
An open pit where rock is cut or dug out from the surface.
quartz
A very common silicate mineral, made of silicon and oxygen. Its crystals are often clear or milky. The World of Crystals

R

radioactive dating
Working out the actual age of a rock, in millions of years, by measuring its radioactivity. Strata
recrystallize
To form new crystals from old ones. In metamorphic rock this happens without melting. Rocks from Inside the Earth
Red Sea
The narrow sea between Africa and Arabia. It is slowly widening as Arabia drifts away from Africa. Drifting Continents
relative age
Whether a rock is older or younger than another, without a date in years. Strata
rift
A long crack where the earth’s shell is splitting apart. Drifting Continents
Ring of Fire
The chain of volcanic islands and mountains around the Pacific Ocean, above sinking plate edges. Volcanoes

S

schist
A metamorphic rock that was once mud. It often contains garnet crystals and flaky mica. Rocks from Inside the Earth
sea level
The average height of the surface of the sea. Heights on land are measured from it, and it changes over long periods. Our Earth
sediment
Sand, mud and other particles carried by wind, water or ice and laid down in layers. The Outer Layers
sedimentary rock
Rock formed from layers of sediment that were laid down and hardened. Strata
seismograph
An instrument that detects and records earthquake waves. Earthquakes
shelf sea
A shallow sea that floods a continental shelf, like the North Sea or the Persian Gulf. The Moving Map
shell
The earth’s rigid outer layer: the crust together with the solid top of the mantle. Inside the Earth
shield volcano
A broad, gently sloping volcano built from great flows of runny lava, as in Hawaii. Volcanoes
shock wave
A wave of energy that travels through rock. Shock waves from earthquakes show scientists what the inside of the earth is like. Inside the Earth
silicate
A mineral made of oxygen and silicon, often joined with metals. Silicates are the largest group of minerals. The World of Crystals
soil
The loose top layer of the land, made of weathered rock mixed with the remains of plants and animals. Our Earth
solar system
The Sun and everything that travels around it, including the eight planets. How the Earth Was Formed
strain
The build-up of stress in rocks that are locked together, until they break. Earthquakes
stratum
A single layer of sedimentary rock. The plural is strata. Strata
stress
A force that squeezes or stretches rock. Too much stress makes rock crack. The Outer Layers
subduction
The sinking of one plate beneath another, back down into the hot mantle. Plates
superposition
The rule that in undisturbed strata, the oldest layer is at the bottom and the youngest is at the top. Strata

T

thin section
A slice of rock ground so thin that light shines through it. Geologists study it under a microscope with crossed polarizers. The World of Crystals
tremor
A small shaking of the ground. Earthquakes
tsunami
A massive sea wave caused by an earthquake or other sudden movement under the sea. Earthquakes

U

U-shaped valley
A valley with steep sides and a wide floor, carved out by a moving glacier. The Moving Map
uplift
The slow raising of land by movements inside the earth. The Moving Map
uranium
A radioactive metal. It can be used as a source of power instead of fossil fuels. Treasures from the Earth

V

volcanic chain
A line of volcanoes formed along the edge of a plate. Mountains
volcanic rock
Rock formed when molten rock from inside the earth reaches the surface and cools.

W

weathering
The breaking down of rock at the surface by wind, rain, ice and living things. Our Earth
white-hot
So hot that it glows white. White-hot is hotter than red-hot.
